EduHackers Research Phase — Recovered History
Purpose
This file reconstructs the key research decisions that shaped V ossie before the EduHackers 2.0 consolidation.
Research framing
The project was researched as more than an e-commerce app. The engineering and product research examined market-
place matching, Uber-style dispatch, campus hubs, seller/courier workflows, identity , payments, realtime state, custody ,
Incubation and cross-campus operations.
T wo application families
A major research decision was to separate the student marketplace experience from staff/operations workflows while keeping
a shared backend.
Marketplace family
• Buyer
• Seller
• Courier
Operations family
• Hub Operator

• Incubation
• Moderator
• Finance
• Support
• Admin
This prevents a student-facing app from becoming cluttered with dense staff tables and prevents staff users from being
given inappropriate marketplace screens or permissions.
Identity research
The research rejected format checks as authentication. A student number beginning with EDUV or an institutional email
domain may be useful validation metadata, but neither proves identity .
The target model became institutional federation:
V ossie → Eduvos/Microsoft sign-in → existing MF A/Authenticator → signed identity result → active-student eligibility
check → V ossie session.
V ossie should not receive the institutional password, Authenticator seed or MF A approval secret.
Progressive roles
The research established that a student should have one identity and progressively activate capabilities:
• Buyer: institutional/student verification.
• Seller: Buyer verification + additional seller onboarding and payout/compliance setup.
• Courier: verified identity + courier onboarding, transport mode and payout setup.
• Staff: assigned membership, stronger MF A/role controls and auditability .
Backend authority
The UI can hide or show actions, but the server remains authoritative. Client-side role selection never grants permission.

Marketplace and dispatch research
Seller matching and courier dispatch should use explicit state machines and atomic acceptance. If two users accept the
same opportunity at the same time, the database must guarantee only one successful lock.
Hub and custody research
Packages become auditable objects. QR scanning changes custody only after server validation. Hub receipt, courier pickup
and buyer handover must be traceable.
Financial research
The system should not mutate a single wallet balance blindly . Financial movements should be append-only ledger entries
with provider references and idempotency keys.
Realtime research
REST/transactional APIs remain the source of truth. W ebSockets/realtime are used for fast updates such as courier
location, seller offers, package scans and order states. After reconnect, the client fetches authoritative state again.
Platform research stack
Research candidates converged around TypeScript, Next.js/React, React Native/Expo for native mobile when needed, T auri
for possible desktop packaging, PostgreSQL/PostGIS, realtime infrastructure and GitHub-based engineering workflow.
Research outcome
The work moved from “marketplace concept” into an implementation system with detailed contracts, screens, APIs, tables,
acceptance criteria and release gates.
