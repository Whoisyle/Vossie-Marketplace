EduHackers 2.0 Decision Session — Consolidated Record
Purpose
This is the decision-oriented reconstruction of the long EduHackers 2.0 working session in which the current V ossie product
policy was refined.
Money model
The pilot model was locked as a working hypothesis:
• Seller commission: 7% of seller transaction value.
• Courier commission: 5% of courier earnings.
• No separate buyer percentage commission in the pilot.
• Buyer pays applicable delivery cost by default.
• Seller/courier can choose automatic payout or manual withdrawal.
• Only A vailable funds can be paid out.
Earnings states:
Pending → Secured → A vailable → Payout Processing → Paid Out.
Delivery and buyer protection
• QR confirms custody transfer.
• Recipient OTP confirms delivery .
• OTP proves delivery , not product satisfaction.
• OTP delivery starts a 24-hour issue window .
• A qualifying dispute freezes affected seller funds.
• Seller gets the first opportunity to resolve.
• Unresolved cases escalate to Operations.
• Courier earnings are not automatically frozen for a product-quality complaint if OTP-confirmed delivery was success-
fully completed.
Returns
Responsibility determines cost:
• Seller fault → seller pays.
• Buyer change-of-mind/buyer fault where allowed → buyer pays.
• Hub/courier fault → logistics incident; buyer and seller should not be charged as though either caused it.
• Unclear responsibility → Operations decision.
Authentication
The group rejected student-number/email-format validation as suﬀicient identity proof.
Production target:
Continue with Eduvos → Microsoft/Entra → existing Authenticator/MF A → signed identity response → active-student
verification → V ossie session.
Prototype target:
Simulate the exact institutional flow clearly as sandbox/demo authentication. Do not imply a live Eduvos integration exists
before formal approval.
No V ossie-specific F ace ID/passkey requirement was retained for the prototype.
Cross-campus transfer
A hybrid model was locked:
• Prefer pooled hub-to-hub movement when route volume justifies it.
• Route through nearest viable campuses, not merely nearest coordinates.
1
• Every leg uses manifests and QR custody .
• Buyer sees one continuous shipment journey .
• Offer a third-party courier alternative when hub pooling is ineﬀicient or too slow.
• Long-distance transfer eligibility is not automatically granted to ordinary local student couriers.
Buyer Requests
Requests became a first-class marketplace capability:
Buyer chooses category , describes need, adds timing/campus/budget and optional media → eligible sellers are notified →
sellers respond with offers → buyer accepts one → request converts into normal order/booking flow.
The product avoids a blind auction race. Offers are structured and comparable.
T wo-app structure
App 1 — Marketplace
Buyer, Seller, Courier.
App 2 — Operations/business side
Campus Hub, T ransfers, Seller Studio where relevant, Incubation and Platform Operations/Admin.
Operations is permissioned, not a universal super-admin experience.
Seller Studio and timetable
Student sellers can upload a timetable PDF or add availability manually . The system extracts class times for convenience,
then the student confirms/edits availability before it becomes authoritative. The original timetable need not be retained
longer than required for the workflow.
Inventory
T op-level categories were kept deliberately limited and expanded with subcategories/attributes:
• F ood & Drinks
• Essentials
• F ashion & Lifestyle
• Services
• Rentals
• Digital
• feature-gated future categories
Category-specific schemas reveal only relevant fields.
Digital inventory
Potential categories include notes, study guides, templates and other legitimate digital products. Past exams or institutional
material require stronger ownership/integrity moderation rather than being automatically accepted.
Listing assets
Sellers fill structured fields; V ossie renders consistent buyer-facing cards/pages. Typical buyer presentation includes im-
age/video, title, seller, price, promotion/special state, description, variants/attributes and category-specific transaction
controls.
Marketplace Reels
Reels were locked as a commerce-discovery tab:
• 15–30 second seller video.
• V ertical swipe feed.
• Linked seller identity .

• Caption.
• Pinned product/service rail.
• Direct product/cart action.
• Seller analytics for views/taps/orders.
• Reporting/moderation.
• No need to become a generic social network.
F ood trust/compliance
Any Seller can use the F ood category; food is not a separate account type. V ossie adds food-specific fields, seller responsibility ,
ingredients/allergens, preparation information, food reviews and incident/reporting paths. The platform should not rely
on “buy at your own risk” as its main legal/trust model.
Communications
One transaction/event timeline drives notifications. Key milestones create in-app/push/email events. Receipts remain
accessible under Orders. Critical transactional communications are not treated as marketing preferences.
Prototype integrations
Prototype and production are separated:
• Maps/GPS: real UI and simulated movement in prototype; approved provider/live device location in production.
• Payments: sandbox/provider adapter in prototype; approved PSP with live merchant credentials in production.
• Identity: simulated institutional flow in prototype; approved Eduvos/Microsoft federation in production.
• Notifications: demo/sandbox delivery in prototype; production provider later.
