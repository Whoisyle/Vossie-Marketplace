Project Context
Origin
V ossie began as an Eduvos student marketplace concept and evolved through the EduHackers research/build process into
a much broader campus-commerce operating system.
The project combines:
• verified student identity ,
• local campus buying and selling,
• student courier participation,
• campus hub custody and collection,
• cross-campus parcel movement,
• seller analytics and incubation support,
• operations/admin tooling,
• payments, disputes and auditability ,
• short-form commerce discovery through Marketplace Reels,
• buyer-side Requests when a needed product/service is not listed.
Core differentiation
The strongest thesis is not “marketplace for students. ” The stronger combination is:
verified campus identity + multi-role student participation + campus logistics/custody + cross-campus
transfers + operations tooling + demand intelligence for student entrepreneurship.
Current project state
The work now exists across strategy PDF s, a 177-screen UI/UX atlas, developer guides, motion specifications, business
research, marketing materials and a cinematic pitch blueprint.
The project should be treated as a system with a canonical hierarchy of decisions. Older prototypes and older PDF s are
useful references, but newer confirmed decisions override them.
Prototype philosophy
The demo should feel real and interactive:
• real state transitions inside the prototype,
• simulated/sandbox external integrations,
• real cross-device responsive behaviour,
• no false claims of live Eduvos, payment-provider or courier-provider connectivity ,
1
• deterministic demo data and reset paths,
• polished motion and microinteractions.
Zero-budget principle
Use free tiers, local simulation and open/free tools for the prototype. Keep all provider-specific integrations behind adapters
so the implementation can switch later without rebuilding the product domain.

Product Vision and Differentiation
Vision
V ossie is a campus-commerce operating system for verified students. It is designed to connect everyday student demand
with student supply , local fulfilment, accountable custody and entrepreneurship support.
Differentiation stack
The defensible idea is the combination of:
1. verified student identity ,
2. Buyer/Seller/Courier participation in one account,
3. campus-local marketplace density ,
4. Request marketplace for unmet demand,
5. Marketplace Reels for commerce discovery ,
6. QR/OTP custody and delivery proof,
7. Campus Hubs and cross-campus transfers,
8. Incubation intelligence from real demand/supply signals,
9. Operations tooling for support, disputes, finance, compliance and security .
Competitive framing
Large e-commerce marketplaces have selection and mature fulfilment but do not optimize for verified campus peer commerce.
Social/DM commerce has discovery but weak transaction state and accountability . Classifieds have broad supply but
not integrated campus identity , custody , courier and incubation. On-demand delivery has strong logistics but is usually
merchant-led rather than peer campus commerce.
Product thesis
V ossie should be pitched as campus commerce infrastructure , not another classifieds board.

Authentication, Eduvos Identity and MF A
Security principle
Identifier format is not proof of identity . An EDUV-prefixed student number or institutional-looking email may be validated
syntactically , but neither grants access by itself.
Production target
V ossie → Continue with Eduvos → Eduvos/Microsoft identity provider → existing Microsoft Authenticator/MF A →
signed identity response → V ossie validates issuer/tenant/token → V ossie verifies active-student eligibility → V ossie session.
V ossie should not receive/store:
• institutional password,
• Authenticator seed,
• MF A approval code.
Separate concepts
• Authentication: control of institutional identity .
• Eligibility: active student allowed to participate.
• Authorization: Buyer/Seller/Courier/staff permissions inside V ossie.
Prototype
Use a clearly marked sandbox simulation:
Continue with Eduvos → demo Microsoft-style sign-in → simulated Authenticator approval → identity verified → cam-
pus/student status displayed → V ossie session.
Never claim the demo is connected to Eduvos production infrastructure.

SOURCE FILE: 03_COMMERCE/01_MONEY_MODEL_LEDGER.md
Pilot Money Model and Ledger
Pilot commercial hypotheses
• Seller commission: 7% of seller transaction value.
• Courier commission: 5% of courier delivery earnings.
• No listing fee or seller subscription in the pilot.
• No separate V ossie buyer percentage fee.
• Buyer pays applicable delivery by default.
These are hypotheses subject to PSP economics, legal/accounting review and pilot evidence.
Earnings states
Pending → Secured → A vailable → Payout Processing → Paid Out
Suggested meaning:
• Pending: order/earning exists but payment state not yet secured.
• Secured: PSP-confirmed payment exists; not yet releasable.
• A vailable: release conditions met.
• Payout Processing: payout command sent/processing.
• Paid Out: provider-confirmed settlement.
Payout preference
Seller/courier chooses:
• Automatic payout, or
• Manual withdrawal.
Only A vailable funds may move.
Ledger principle
Do not edit a single balance blindly . Use append-only/reference-based ledger entries with idempotency keys and provider
references.

Disputes, Refunds and Returns
Delivery vs satisfaction
Recipient OTP proves delivery, not product satisfaction.
After OTP-confirmed delivery , the buyer receives a 24-hour issue window .
Dispute flow
1. Buyer opens issue within allowed window.
2. Structured evidence is attached to the order case.
3. Affected seller funds are paused/frozen as appropriate.
4. Seller gets first opportunity to resolve within defined response policy .
5. Unresolved case escalates to Operations.
6. Operations reviews order data, chat/evidence, QR custody , OTP , payment and timeline.
7. Refund/partial refund/denial/return outcome is recorded and processed through the PSP adapter.
12
Return-cost responsibility
• Seller fault → seller bears return cost.
• Buyer change of mind / buyer fault where returns are allowed → buyer bears return cost.
• Courier/hub fault → logistics incident; buyer/seller should not automatically bear the cost.
• Unclear case → Operations adjudicates.
Courier earnings
A later product-quality dispute should not automatically freeze courier earnings when OTP-confirmed delivery was valid
and courier service was not the cause.

SOURCE FILE: 04_LOGISTICS/03_CROSS_CAMPUS_TRANSFERS.md
Cross-Campus T ransfers
Hybrid model
Cross-campus is not a single student driving across provinces for every order.
Preferred model:
• campus hub network for pooled routes,
• third-party courier fallback for low-volume/long-distance cases.
Routing
F or hub-to-hub movement, route through the nearest viable campuses, not merely nearest by straight-line distance.
Viability includes route direction, schedule, capacity and operational availability .
Example concept:
Cape T own-area campus → next viable campus hub(s) → destination region/campus → local fulfilment.
16
User experience
Buyer sees one shipment and one tracking timeline, not many fragmented orders.
Operational model
Each leg has a manifest and custody scans. Intermediate hubs reconcile packages before forwarding.
Third-party fallback
If hub transfer is not economical (e.g. one parcel), offer approved external logistics such as parcel-point/direct courier where
available. Third-party provider should be integrated through an adapter so the V ossie order still has a unified status view.
