V ossie Project Origin and Evolution
Purpose
This file preserves the project history from the earliest EduHackers/V ossie discussions through the current EduHackers 2.0
architecture so a fresh account can understand how the product evolved and why the present decisions exist.
Earliest product direction
V ossie began as an Eduvos student marketplace. The first idea was simple: students should be able to buy and sell products
and services around campus more easily than through fragmented WhatsApp groups, DMs, social pages or informal word
of mouth.
The project quickly expanded because a simple listings app did not solve the hard parts: trust, verification, fulfilment,
student logistics, cross-campus movement, support, disputes, business growth and campus oversight.
Brand and interaction direction
The visual direction was intentionally separated from the functionality model:
• Plato-inspired visual literacy: soft colours, warm spacing, clean photography , gentle panels, restrained typography
and a premium-but-approachable campus feel.
• Uber-inspired interaction literacy: task clarity , live status, cards, sheets, maps, request/accept patterns, activity
history , rapid actions and obvious next steps.
• V ossie must remain its own brand and must not copy proprietary Plato or Uber assets or exact layouts.
Platform expansion
The project evolved from one marketplace into two application families sharing one backend:
1. V ossie Marketplace— Buyer, Seller and Courier under one student identity .
2. V ossie Operations — Campus Hub, T ransfers, Incubation, Support, Moderation, Finance, Compliance, Security ,
Admin and Platform Operations.
Major concepts added over time
The project added, in roughly this evolutionary order:
• Buyer, Seller and Courier role model.
• Student identity and Eduvos verification.
• Campus pickup and delivery .
• Hub custody and QR scanning.
• Courier OTP handover.
• Cross-campus transfers.
• Third-party courier fallback.
• Buyer Requests when no suitable listing exists.
• Dynamic listing categories and subcategories.
• Digital study materials and controlled digital delivery .
• F ood marketplace trust/compliance handling.
• Seller Studio and timetable-based availability .
• Incubation intelligence for student businesses.
• Platform Operations command centre.
• Payments, ledger, fees, payouts, refunds and disputes.
• Receipts, notifications and email event templates.
• Marketplace Reels tied to actual commerce objects.
• PW A/universal link distribution.
• Figma design-system production.
• Motion and microinteraction system.
• Cursor + VS Code agentic development workflow.
• Cinematic Apple-style pitch system.
• Business analysis, market research and marketing campaign system.

Current product thesis
V ossie is not merely a student marketplace. It is a verified campus commerce, logistics, operations and student-
entrepreneurship platform .
Its differentiation comes from the combination of:
• verified campus identity ,
• multi-role student participation,
• listings + requests + Reels discovery ,
• student courier fulfilment,
• QR custody and campus hubs,
• cross-campus logistics,
• operational tooling,
• seller analytics,
• Incubation intelligence,
• auditable payments/disputes,
• and institution-facing visibility .
Current scale of the specification
The latest engineering handoff describes a large system surface: 90 database tables, 202 API operations, 177 screen designs,
514 action bindings and 385 delivery tickets. This is a specification universe, not a claim that every element must ship in
the first pilot.
Current prototype philosophy
The prototype should prove complete vertical slices instead of showing hundreds of disconnected screens. The strongest
journey is:
Authentication → Buyer Marketplace → Reels/Listings/Requests → Checkout → Payment state → Seller → Courier →
QR custody → OTP delivery → Hub/Cross-campus → Incubation/Operations.
Project constraint
The prototype is intentionally zero-budget wherever possible. Paid provider dependencies must be optional, simulated,
replaceable or deferred until a real production pilot is approved.
